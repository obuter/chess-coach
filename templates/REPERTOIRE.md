# Repertoire — six moves, no theory

This exists so you never think on moves 1–6. It is **not** something to study. At
beginner level, what costs you points in the first ten moves is almost never theory.
It is the free material your opponent hands you and you walk past. See *Taking what
they give you*, below.

Play these moves in this order, adjusting only when something is actually attacked.

## As White: 1.e4 and the Italian setup

**e4 · Nf3 · Bc4 · d3 · c3 · O-O**

What you are trying to do: bishop on c4 aims at f7, the weakest square in their
position. d3 and c3 keep the bishop's diagonal open and prepare d4 later. Castle,
then look for the first free capture.

If they play 1...e5, the move order above is exact. If they play something else
(1...c5, 1...d5, 1...e6), play the same pieces to the same squares anyway.

## As Black vs 1.e4: mirror it

**e5 · Nc6 · Bc5 · d6 · Nf6 · O-O**

Knights before bishops. Bishop to c5 pointing at their f2.

## As Black vs 1.d4: the fianchetto

**Nf6 · g6 · Bg7 · O-O · d6 · Nc6**

Bishop on g7 stares down the long diagonal at their queenside. Castle early.

## Taking what they give you

At beginner level opponents give away a pawn or a piece in the first five moves
constantly. Add your own examples here as the reviews find them — write the
opponent's name followed by the move you should have played (e.g. `vs alice … 3.Nxe5`)
and the dashboard links straight to that moment in the game.

So: **finish your six moves, but look at every capture first.** The repertoire is
the default, not an obligation. A free pawn beats a developing move every time.

## The only two traps worth knowing

**Scholar's mate.** If their queen comes out early and a bishop sits on c4, they are
aiming at f7. Which answer is right depends on where the queen is:

- **Queen on h5** (the usual one): play **`...g6`** or **`...Qe7`**. Do *not* play
  `...d5` here — `Bxd5` just takes it (Stockfish: `...g6` +0.3, `...d5` −1.9).
- **Queen on f5**: **`...d5`**. The pawn hits the bishop and your c8 bishop hits the
  queen.

**The f7 / f2 fork.** A knight reaching g5 (or g4 against you) combined with a bishop
on c4 attacks f7 twice. Count the defenders of f7 before you let a knight land on g5.

## When you leave the repertoire

That is fine and it will happen most games. From move 6 onward there is no book —
just the scan: *what can I take, what can they take, any checks?*
